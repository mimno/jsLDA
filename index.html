    How it works

    What is topic modeling?

    A topic model finds groups of words that tend to appear together in the same documents. Each group is a “topic”. Every document is described as a mix of topics. This page uses latent Dirichlet allocation (LDA), trained by Gibbs sampling: it starts with every word in a random topic, then repeatedly revisits each word and reassigns it based on the other words around it.

    Preparing your texts

    • Plain text only. Convert PDFs and Word files to .txt first. Other files in a folder are skipped.
    • Length matters. Topic models work best on passages of a paragraph or a few pages. Longer texts are split into passages automatically (see Options), named moby-dick-1.txt, moby-dick-2.txt, and so on.
    • One document per line. You can also open a single file where each line is id label text, separated by tabs, as used by MALLET.
    • Metadata. Documents can carry a label: the second column of a one-document-per-line file, or the subfolder a file is in (fiction/moby-dick.txt is labeled fiction). With labels, a “By metadata” tab compares topics across them: as a time line if the labels are years or dates (like 1914 or 2020-03-15), otherwise as bar charts.
    • Languages. Words are split on spaces and punctuation, so languages written without spaces (such as Chinese or Japanese) need to be segmented into words first.

    Reading the results

    Topics are listed by their top words, most common first. Click a topic to see the documents that use it most. If a topic is full of uninteresting words, remove them in the Vocabulary tab; training continues without them. Try a few different numbers of topics: there is no single right answer.

    Privacy

    Everything runs in this browser tab. Your files are read by the page but never sent anywhere, and closing the tab discards them. Save anything you want to keep from the Save results tab.
